Quick Review: ...So Far

    Over the first three weeks, you already have a good foundation in core programming practices. You have:
  • Written programs using sequential processing to execute steps in order
  • Used conditional logic to make decisions using if, elif, and else
  • Created loops to perform iteration and automate repetitive tasks
  • Designed modular programs using custom functions to break logic into reusable pieces
  • Practiced testing and debugging to refine and improve your code
  • And most recently, you’ve begun working with simple data collections such as lists and tuples

This is a lot of material in a short time, and you should be proud of the skills you are developing.

I've been especially encouraged by the collaborative discussions and efforts I've seen on our Discord channel.

My students often hear me say that I want you to be challenged and frustrated at times and working through those challenges is when the real learning happens. But let me be clear. I want you to be frustrated, but I don't want you to stay frustrated! How long is too long? That's a very personal question and it will absolutely vary from person to person and even from time to time. For some, it may be 15 minutes. For others, it may be an hour or more. If you are stuck and frustrated, how do you deal with it? For me, I shift gears and work on something else, or walk away for a bit, or ask for help. When I come back to the problem, I have a fresh perspective and often see things that I missed before.
